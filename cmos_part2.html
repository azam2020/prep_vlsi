Chapter 3: CMOS & VLSI Basics — Part 2 (Q51 to Q100)

Power Dissipation, Delay Modeling (Logical Effort), and Short Channel Effects

5. Power Dissipation (Dynamic & Static)

Q51 What are the three main components of power dissipation in a CMOS circuit?

Answer:
1. Dynamic Power (Switching Power): Charging and discharging the load capacitance.
2. Short-Circuit Power: Current flowing directly from $V_{DD}$ to GND during transitions.
3. Static Power (Leakage): Current flowing when the circuit is entirely idle/stable.

Q52 Write the fundamental equation for Dynamic (Switching) Power.
$P_{dyn} = \alpha \cdot C_{load} \cdot V_{DD}^2 \cdot f_{clk}$

Where $\alpha$ is the activity factor, $C_{load}$ is the load capacitance, $V_{DD}$ is supply voltage, and $f_{clk}$ is the clock frequency.

Q53 What is the Activity Factor ($\alpha$)?

Answer: It is the probability that a node will transition from 0 to 1 during a given clock cycle. Since power is only drawn from the supply when charging the capacitor (0 to 1), a node that transitions every single cycle has an $\alpha$ of 1.

Q54 Tricky What is the activity factor ($\alpha$) of a Clock signal versus a Data signal?

Answer:
• Clock Signal: $\alpha = 1$. It rises exactly once per cycle, every cycle.
• Data Signal: $\alpha$ is typically estimated around $0.1$ to $0.2$ for general logic, but can be higher or lower depending on the data pattern and logic depth.

Concept: Because $\alpha=1$ and it drives massive capacitance, the Clock Tree consumes up to 40% of the total dynamic power in an SoC. This is why Clock Gating is the most effective power-saving technique.
Q55 Whiteboard During a 0-to-1 transition of an inverter, how much total energy is drawn from the $V_{DD}$ supply, and how much is stored in the load capacitor?

Answer:
• Total energy drawn from supply = $C V_{DD}^2$.
• Energy actually stored in the capacitor = $\frac{1}{2} C V_{DD}^2$.

Q56 Where does the other half of the energy go during the 0-to-1 transition?

Answer: The missing $\frac{1}{2} C V_{DD}^2$ is dissipated as heat in the resistance of the PMOS transistor as the current flows through it to charge the capacitor.

Q57 During a 1-to-0 transition, how much energy is drawn from $V_{DD}$?

Answer: Zero. During a falling transition, the NMOS turns on and discharges the capacitor to Ground. The $\frac{1}{2} C V_{DD}^2$ of energy that was stored in the capacitor is now dissipated as heat in the NMOS. No new energy is pulled from the power supply.

Q58 What determines the magnitude of Short-Circuit Power?

Answer: Short-circuit power ($P_{sc} = I_{sc} V_{DD} f$) is dominated by the input transition time (slew rate). If the input rises very slowly, both the PMOS and NMOS remain ON simultaneously for a longer period, creating a direct path from $V_{DD}$ to GND, burning massive power.

Q59 What are the three main components of Static (Leakage) Power?

Answer:
1. Subthreshold Leakage ($I_{sub}$): Current flowing from Drain to Source even when $V_{GS} < V_{TH}$.
2. Gate Oxide Leakage ($I_{gate}$): Quantum tunneling current straight through the ultra-thin gate oxide.
3. Junction Leakage ($I_{junc}$): Reverse-biased p-n diode leakage between the source/drain diffusions and the body substrate.

Q60 Why does Subthreshold Leakage increase exponentially in modern technology nodes?

Answer: To maintain performance while lowering $V_{DD}$ (to save dynamic power), engineers must also lower $V_{TH}$. However, subthreshold leakage has an exponential dependence on $V_{TH}$ ($I_{sub} \propto e^{-V_{TH}/V_T}$). A small drop in threshold voltage causes a massive explosion in off-state leakage.

Q61 How did the semiconductor industry solve Gate Oxide leakage scaling past 45nm?

Answer: They introduced HKMG (High-K Metal Gate). Standard $SiO_2$ had to be made so thin (sub-2nm) that electrons quantum-tunneled straight through it. By using a High-K dielectric (like Hafnium Oxide), they could make the oxide physically thicker (stopping tunneling) while maintaining the exact same electrical capacitance for channel control.

Q62 What is Clock Gating?

Answer: A technique to reduce Dynamic Power. An Integrated Clock Gating (ICG) cell uses an enable signal to turn off the clock to a specific block of logic when it is idle. This drops the Activity Factor ($\alpha$) to 0, saving massive $CV^2f$ power.

Q63 What is Power Gating?

Answer: A technique to reduce Static (Leakage) Power. High-Vt "Sleep Transistors" are placed between the logic block and the power rails ($V_{DD}$ or GND). When the block is idle, the sleep transistors are turned off, physically severing the block from the power supply.

Q64 Tricky What is a Retention Register, and why is it needed in Power Gating?

Answer: When you Power Gate a block, all its internal Flip-Flops lose power and their stored data dies. A Retention Register is a special flip-flop connected to an "always-on" power rail. It saves the state of critical variables right before sleep, and restores them instantly upon wake-up without requiring a full system reboot.

Q65 What is Multi-Vt Synthesis?

Answer: Using transistors with different Threshold Voltages on the same chip.
• Low-Vt (LVT): Fast, but leaks heavily. Used ONLY on critical timing paths.
• High-Vt (HVT): Slow, but very low leakage. Used on non-critical paths to aggressively save static power.

Q66 What is DVFS?

Answer: Dynamic Voltage and Frequency Scaling. The SoC contains a power controller that actively monitors workload. If a task is simple (reading an e-book), it drops both the clock frequency AND the supply voltage, yielding a cubic power reduction (since $P \propto V^2 f$).

Q67 What is a Level Shifter, and when is it required?

Answer: When using Multi-Vdd (different voltage domains on one chip), a signal crossing from a lower voltage domain (e.g., 0.8V) to a higher voltage domain (e.g., 1.2V) cannot fully turn off the high-domain PMOS, causing massive short-circuit leakage. A Level Shifter is a specialized circuit that safely translates the 0.8V signal up to a 1.2V swing.

Q68 Does replacing all inverters in a design with NAND gates save power?

Answer: No, it increases power and area. NAND gates have higher input capacitance and use more transistors (4 vs 2), meaning higher $C_{load}$ and more switching power.

Q69 How does temperature affect leakage power?

Answer: Subthreshold leakage increases exponentially with temperature. This creates a dangerous positive feedback loop (Thermal Runaway): higher temp $\rightarrow$ higher leakage $\rightarrow$ more heat generated $\rightarrow$ even higher leakage, eventually melting the chip if not throttled.

Q70 How does substrate biasing (Body Biasing) reduce leakage?

Answer: By intentionally applying a negative voltage to the NMOS body ($V_B < 0V$), the Body Effect is weaponized to artificially increase the threshold voltage ($V_{TH}$) during standby mode, exponentially dropping the subthreshold leakage current.

6. Propagation Delay, Fan-Out, and Logical Effort

Q71 Define Propagation Delay ($t_{pd}$).

Answer: The time required for a signal to propagate through a gate. It is measured from the 50% point of the input transition to the 50% point of the output transition.

Q72 What is the difference between $t_{pHL}$ and $t_{pLH}$?

Answer:
• $t_{pHL}$: Delay when the output transitions from High to Low (discharging via NMOS).
• $t_{pLH}$: Delay when the output transitions from Low to High (charging via PMOS).

Q73 Write the first-order RC delay equation for a gate.
$t_{pd} \approx 0.69 \cdot R_{eq} \cdot C_{load}$

Where $R_{eq}$ is the equivalent ON-resistance of the driving transistor, and $C_{load}$ is the total load capacitance.

Q74 What does a gate's Total Load Capacitance ($C_{load}$) consist of?

Answer:
1. Intrinsic: Diffusion (drain) capacitance of the driving gate itself.
2. Extrinsic: Wire routing capacitance AND the Gate capacitance of the next stage (Fan-out).

Q75 What is Elmore Delay used for?

Answer: It is an analytical method used to estimate the delay through a complex RC tree (like a long routing wire with multiple branches). It calculates delay by summing the resistance of each segment multiplied by the downstream capacitance it drives.

Q76 Tricky Why does the delay of a long wire scale quadratically ($L^2$) with its length? How do we fix this?

Answer: As wire length ($L$) doubles, its Resistance ($R$) doubles AND its Capacitance ($C$) doubles. Since Delay $\propto R \times C$, the delay quadruples ($L^2$).
Fix: We cut the long wire into smaller segments and insert Repeaters (Buffers) between them. This breaks the $L^2$ curve and makes the delay scale linearly with distance.

Q77 Define Fan-Out.

Answer: The number of identical subsequent logic gates that the output of a driving gate is connected to.

Q78 What is FO4 (Fan-Out of 4), and why is it a crucial metric?

Answer: FO4 is the delay of a standard inverter driving exactly 4 identical copies of itself. It is used as a technology-independent speed metric. If an architecture has a critical path of 15 FO4 delays, engineers can easily compare its expected speed across different technology nodes (e.g., TSMC 7nm vs 3nm).

Q79 What is Logical Effort ($g$)?

Answer: It is the ratio of the input capacitance of a gate to the input capacitance of a standard inverter that provides the exact same output drive current. (An inverter has $g = 1$). It represents how much "worse" a complex gate is at driving a load compared to an inverter.

Q80 What is Electrical Effort ($h$)?

Answer: Also known as Fan-out, it is the ratio of the external load capacitance to the gate's own input capacitance. $h = \frac{C_{out}}{C_{in}}$.

Q81 Write the linear delay equation using Logical Effort.
$d = (g \times h) + p$

Delay ($d$) = Stage Effort ($g \times h$) + Parasitic Delay ($p$).

Q82 Whiteboard Calculate the Logical Effort ($g$) of a 2-input NAND gate vs a 2-input NOR gate. Which is faster?

Answer: (Assuming symmetric reference inverter has NMOS W=1, PMOS W=2, total $C_{in} = 3$).
• 2-input NAND: Series NMOS must be sized 2x (W=2). Parallel PMOS stay W=2. Total $C_{in} = 2+2 = 4$. Logical Effort $g = \frac{4}{3}$.
• 2-input NOR: Series PMOS must be sized 2x (W=4). Parallel NMOS stay W=1. Total $C_{in} = 4+1 = 5$. Logical Effort $g = \frac{5}{3}$.
Conclusion: NAND ($g=4/3$) has a lower logical effort than NOR ($g=5/3$), proving NAND is intrinsically faster.

Q83 How do you minimize delay when driving a massive load (like a clock pin or off-chip pad)?

Answer: By using a progressively sized buffer chain (Horn architecture). Each inverter in the chain is sized larger than the previous one by a constant tapering factor ($f$).

Q84 What is the theoretically optimal tapering factor ($e$) for a buffer chain to achieve absolute minimum delay?

Answer: The mathematical optimum is Euler's number ($e \approx 2.718$). However, because adding more stages increases area and parasitic delay, the practical industry optimum is usually chosen around 4 (hence the popularity of the FO4 metric).

Q85 If you have a gate driving a large capacitance, and you double the width (W) of the gate, what happens to the delay?

Answer: The delay decreases because the ON-resistance ($R_{eq}$) is halved, doubling the drive current. However, it does not perfectly halve the delay because increasing W also increases the gate's internal parasitic diffusion capacitance ($C_{int}$).

7. Short Channel Effects & Sub-micron Physics

Q86 What classifies as a "Short Channel Effect" (SCE)?

Answer: Any physical phenomenon where a transistor deviates from ideal long-channel square-law equations. This occurs when the channel length ($L$) becomes comparable to the depletion region thicknesses of the source and drain.

Q87 What is Velocity Saturation?

Answer: In long channels, electron velocity increases linearly with the horizontal electric field ($E = V_{DS}/L$). In short channels, the field is so intense that electrons collide with the silicon lattice (scattering), and their velocity caps out at a maximum limit ($v_{sat} \approx 10^7$ cm/s).
Impact: Current $I_D$ becomes linearly proportional to $(V_{GS} - V_{TH})$, rather than quadratic!

Q88 What causes Mobility Degradation in short channels?

Answer: To turn on short channel devices, the vertical electric field from the gate ($V_{GS}$) is immense. This pulls electrons violently toward the silicon-oxide interface. As they travel from source to drain, they bounce against the rough interface (Surface Scattering), severely reducing their effective mobility ($\mu$).

Q89 Explain DIBL (Drain Induced Barrier Lowering) in detail.

Answer: The Source and Channel form a potential barrier that stops electrons when the transistor is off. In short channels, the Drain is physically so close to the Source that a high $V_{DS}$ projects its electric field all the way to the Source, artificially lowering that potential barrier.
Result: $V_{TH}$ drops, and leakage current skyrockets even when $V_{GS}=0$.

Q90 What is Punchthrough?

Answer: An extreme case of DIBL. When $V_{DS}$ is very high, the depletion region around the Drain expands so much that it physically touches the depletion region of the Source. The gate completely loses control, and massive, destructive current shoots through the substrate.

Q91 What is Hot Carrier Injection (HCI)?

Answer: Due to the massive electric field near the drain in short channels, electrons accelerate to extreme kinetic energies ("hot" carriers). Some gain enough energy to physically shoot up and embed themselves into the gate oxide.
Result: Over years of use, trapped electrons permanently increase $V_{TH}$, slowing down the chip (Aging/Degradation).

Q92 How does LDD (Lightly Doped Drain) prevent HCI?

Answer: Instead of an abrupt, heavily doped N+ drain, a gradual, lightly doped N- region is placed between the channel and the drain. This spreads out the voltage drop, reducing the peak electric field and preventing electrons from getting "hot" enough to inject into the oxide.

Q93 What is $V_{TH}$ Roll-off?

Answer: As channel length ($L$) decreases, the source and drain depletion regions support some of the bulk charge that the gate was supposed to support (Charge Sharing). Because the gate has less charge to deplete, the Threshold Voltage ($V_{TH}$) drops (rolls off) steeply as $L$ gets smaller.

Q94 What is Halo Doping (Pocket Implants)?

Answer: To prevent punchthrough and counteract $V_{TH}$ roll-off, foundries implant a high concentration of P-type dopants right at the edges of the N+ source and drain. This prevents the depletion regions from expanding into the channel.

Q95 What is the Reverse Short Channel Effect (RSCE)?

Answer: A strange anomaly caused by Halo Doping. As $L$ shrinks, the highly doped Halo regions from the source and drain begin to overlap in the middle of the channel. The average channel doping increases, causing the $V_{TH}$ to actually increase before it eventually rolls off.

Q96 What is NBTI (Negative Bias Temperature Instability)?

Answer: The dominant aging mechanism in PMOS transistors. When a PMOS is strongly ON (negative $V_{GS}$) at high temperatures, bonds break at the Silicon-Oxide interface, trapping positive charge. Over months/years, the $|V_{TP}|$ increases, making the PMOS slower. (Foundries must add "guard bands" to timing analysis to account for 10-year NBTI degradation).

Q97 What is GIDL (Gate Induced Drain Leakage)?

Answer: When the gate is at 0V and the drain is at high $V_{DD}$, an immense vertical electric field exists in the overlap region between the gate and the drain. This field causes electrons to quantum-tunnel directly from the valence band to the conduction band (band-to-band tunneling), causing leakage straight into the substrate.

Q98 Why did traditional Planar CMOS completely stop scaling around 20nm?

Answer: At 20nm, the channel is so short that the gate, sitting only on top of the silicon, completely lost electrostatic control of the channel. Leakage (DIBL and Subthreshold) could no longer be stopped by Halo doping. The gate could not "pinch off" the leakage path deep inside the substrate.

Q99 What is a FinFET, and how did it save Moore's Law?

Answer: A FinFET is a 3D transistor where the silicon channel is raised into a vertical "fin". The gate material is draped over the fin, controlling the channel from three sides (top, left, right). This massive increase in electrostatic control eliminated deep-substrate leakage, restoring low $V_{TH}$ operation and saving scaling down to 3nm.

Q100 Tricky Can a Physical Design Engineer draw a FinFET with a width (W) of 1.5$\mu m$?

Answer: NO. Width is Quantized in FinFETs. You cannot draw arbitrary widths anymore. The width of a single fin is fixed by the foundry (e.g., $W_{fin} = 2 \times H_{fin} + T_{fin}$). To increase drive strength, you can only add discrete integer numbers of fins. You can have a 2-fin device, or a 3-fin device, but nothing in between.

Concept: This quantized sizing fundamentally changed how custom analog circuits and SRAM cells are designed in sub-14nm nodes.
