Chapter 3: CMOS & VLSI Basics — Part 1 (Q1 to Q50)

MOSFET Operation, Body Effect, CMOS Inverter, VTC Curve, and Noise Margins

1. MOSFET Fundamentals & Operation

Q1 What does CMOS stand for, and why did it replace NMOS-only logic?

Answer: CMOS stands for Complementary Metal-Oxide-Semiconductor. It uses both NMOS and PMOS transistors in a complementary pull-down/pull-up network. It replaced NMOS logic because CMOS has theoretically zero static power dissipation (except for leakage) since there is no direct DC path from $V_{DD}$ to GND in a steady state.

Q2 What are the majority charge carriers in NMOS vs PMOS?

Answer:
• NMOS: Electrons.
• PMOS: Holes.

Q3 Why is an NMOS transistor faster than a PMOS transistor of the exact same size?

Answer: Because the mobility ($\mu_n$) of electrons (majority carriers in NMOS) is approximately 2 to 3 times higher than the mobility ($\mu_p$) of holes (majority carriers in PMOS) through silicon. Electrons travel faster, meaning NMOS can discharge a capacitor much faster than an identically sized PMOS can charge it.

Q4 Define Threshold Voltage ($V_{TH}$).

Answer: It is the minimum Gate-to-Source voltage ($V_{GS}$) required to create a conducting inversion channel between the Source and Drain regions.

Q5 What are the three surface states of a MOS Capacitor as gate voltage increases?

Answer:
1. Accumulation: ($V_G < 0$) Holes accumulate at the oxide-substrate interface.
2. Depletion: ($V_G > 0$ but $< V_{TH}$) Holes are pushed away, leaving immobile negative acceptor ions (depletion region).
3. Inversion: ($V_G \ge V_{TH}$) Electrons are attracted to the surface, forming an N-type conductive channel in a P-type substrate.

Q6 Name the three operating regions of an NMOS transistor and their voltage conditions.

Answer:
1. Cutoff: $V_{GS} < V_{TH}$
2. Linear (Triode): $V_{GS} \ge V_{TH}$ AND $V_{DS} < V_{GS} - V_{TH}$
3. Saturation (Active): $V_{GS} \ge V_{TH}$ AND $V_{DS} \ge V_{GS} - V_{TH}$

Q7 What is the equation for Drain Current ($I_D$) in the Cutoff region?

Answer: Ideally, $I_D = 0$. (In reality, there is subthreshold leakage, which we cover later).

Q8 What is the equation for Drain Current ($I_D$) in the Linear (Triode) region?
$I_D = \mu_n C_{ox} \frac{W}{L} \left[ (V_{GS} - V_{TH})V_{DS} - \frac{V_{DS}^2}{2} \right]$

Concept: The transistor acts like a voltage-controlled resistor.

Q9 What is the equation for Drain Current ($I_D$) in the Saturation region?
$I_D = \frac{1}{2} \mu_n C_{ox} \frac{W}{L} (V_{GS} - V_{TH})^2 (1 + \lambda V_{DS})$

Concept: The transistor acts like a voltage-controlled current source. Current is mostly independent of $V_{DS}$ (ignoring Channel Length Modulation $\lambda$).

Q10 What does $C_{ox}$ represent, and what is its formula?

Answer: $C_{ox}$ is the gate oxide capacitance per unit area.
Formula: $C_{ox} = \frac{\epsilon_{ox}}{t_{ox}}$ (where $\epsilon_{ox}$ is the permittivity of the oxide and $t_{ox}$ is the oxide thickness). As nodes shrink, $t_{ox}$ decreases, increasing $C_{ox}$.

Q11 What is Pinch-off?

Answer: Pinch-off occurs when $V_{DS}$ reaches $V_{GS} - V_{TH}$ (the saturation condition). The voltage difference between the gate and the drain drops below the threshold voltage, causing the inversion channel to disappear (pinch off) right at the drain end. Electrons are swept across this depletion region by the electric field.

Q12 Tricky If the channel is "pinched off", how does current still flow from source to drain?

Answer: Pinch-off does not mean the current stops. It means the conductive channel ends before touching the drain. Electrons travel through the channel, reach the pinch-off point, and are instantly swept across the remaining depletion region by the strong electric field generated by $V_{DS}$ (similar to a waterfall).

Q13 Define Overdrive Voltage ($V_{OV}$).

Answer: $V_{OV} = V_{GS} - V_{TH}$. It represents how much extra voltage is applied to the gate beyond what is needed to simply turn the transistor on.

Q14 What happens to an NMOS transistor if it is forced to pass a Logic 1 ($V_{DD}$)?

Answer: NMOS passes a Poor 1. As the source voltage rises toward $V_{DD}$, the $V_{GS}$ drops. When the source reaches $V_{DD} - V_{TH}$, $V_{GS}$ exactly equals $V_{TH}$. The transistor cuts off. Thus, the maximum voltage an NMOS can pass is $V_{DD} - V_{TH}$.

Q15 What happens to a PMOS transistor if it is forced to pass a Logic 0 (GND)?

Answer: PMOS passes a Poor 0. It will stop conducting when the output drops to $|V_{TP}|$. This is why CMOS requires PMOS in the pull-up network (passes strong 1) and NMOS in the pull-down network (passes strong 0).

2. Body Effect & Threshold Variations

Q16 What are the 4 terminals of a MOSFET?

Answer: Gate (G), Source (S), Drain (D), and Body/Bulk (B).

Q17 What is the Body Effect?

Answer: The Body Effect (or back-gate effect) occurs when the Source and Body (substrate) are NOT at the same voltage ($V_{SB} > 0$). This potential difference widens the depletion region under the channel, making it harder to invert the surface, which increases the Threshold Voltage ($V_{TH}$).

Q18 Write the Body Effect Equation.
$V_{TH} = V_{TH0} + \gamma \left( \sqrt{|2\Phi_F + V_{SB}|} - \sqrt{|2\Phi_F|} \right)$

Where $V_{TH0}$ is the threshold at $V_{SB}=0$, $\gamma$ is the body-effect parameter, and $\Phi_F$ is the surface potential.

Q19 Tricky In a stack of three NMOS transistors in series (NAND gate), which transistor suffers the most from the Body Effect?

Answer: The topmost NMOS (the one closest to the output). All NMOS bodies are tied to GND ($0V$). The bottom NMOS has its source tied to GND ($V_{SB} = 0$, no body effect). The middle and top NMOS have their sources connected to intermediate nodes which sit at higher voltages ($V_{SB} > 0$). The top NMOS has the highest $V_S$, thus the highest $V_{SB}$, and the highest threshold voltage.

Q20 To prevent the body effect, what must you do with the Bulk terminals in a CMOS layout?

Answer:
• NMOS bulk must be tied to the lowest potential (GND).
• PMOS bulk must be tied to the highest potential ($V_{DD}$).

Q21 What is Channel Length Modulation (CLM)?

Answer: In ideal saturation, $I_D$ is constant. In reality, as $V_{DS}$ increases beyond pinch-off, the depletion region at the drain expands, physically shortening the effective length of the channel ($L_{eff}$). Since $I_D$ is inversely proportional to $L$, $I_D$ increases slightly as $V_{DS}$ increases in saturation.

Q22 How is CLM represented in the current equation?

Answer: By multiplying the saturation equation by $(1 + \lambda V_{DS})$, where $\lambda$ is the channel-length modulation parameter.

Q23 What does a higher $\lambda$ mean for the output resistance ($R_{out}$) of a transistor in saturation?

Answer: $R_{out} \approx \frac{1}{\lambda I_D}$. A higher $\lambda$ means a steeper slope in the $I_D$-$V_{DS}$ saturation curve, which results in a lower output resistance.

Q24 Define DIBL (Drain-Induced Barrier Lowering).

Answer: As channel lengths get very short (deep sub-micron), a high voltage at the Drain ($V_{DS}$) creates an electric field that reaches all the way to the Source, lowering the potential barrier that prevents electrons from flowing. This effectively lowers the Threshold Voltage ($V_{TH}$), causing increased leakage current even when $V_{GS} = 0$.

Q25 Contrast Body Effect and DIBL.

Answer:
• Body Effect ($V_{SB} > 0$) increases $V_{TH}$.
• DIBL ($V_{DS} > 0$ in short channels) decreases $V_{TH}$.

3. The CMOS Inverter & VTC Curve

Voltage Transfer Characteristic (VTC) of CMOS Inverter
Q26 Draw/Describe the schematic of a CMOS Inverter.

Answer: A PMOS transistor is connected between $V_{DD}$ (Source) and the Output (Drain). An NMOS transistor is connected between the Output (Drain) and GND (Source). The Input is connected to the Gates of both transistors simultaneously.

Q27 What is a Voltage Transfer Characteristic (VTC) curve?

Answer: A plot of the DC Output Voltage ($V_{out}$) versus the DC Input Voltage ($V_{in}$). It shows how the inverter translates input levels to output levels.

Q28 In VTC Region A ($V_{in} < V_{TN}$), what is the state of the transistors?

Answer:
• NMOS: Cutoff (OFF) because $V_{in} < V_{TH}$.
• PMOS: Linear (ON). Since no current flows, $V_{DS\_pmos} = 0$, so $V_{out} = V_{DD}$.

Q29 In VTC Region B ($V_{in} > V_{TN}$, but $V_{out}$ is still high), what is the state?

Answer:
• NMOS: Saturation. It just turned on, and $V_{DS}$ (which is $V_{out}$) is very high, satisfying $V_{DS} > V_{GS} - V_{TN}$.
• PMOS: Linear. $V_{out}$ is close to $V_{DD}$, so its $V_{SD}$ is very small.

Q30 Tricky In VTC Region C (the steep transition region, $V_{in} \approx V_{DD}/2$), what is the state?

Answer:
• NMOS: Saturation.
• PMOS: Saturation.
This is the most critical region! Both transistors are heavily ON and saturated. A massive Short Circuit Current flows directly from $V_{DD}$ to GND.

Q31 In VTC Region D ($V_{in}$ high, $V_{out}$ low), what is the state?

Answer:
• NMOS: Linear. $V_{out}$ has dropped, so $V_{DS}$ is small.
• PMOS: Saturation. It is barely on.

Q32 In VTC Region E ($V_{in} > V_{DD} - |V_{TP}|$), what is the state?

Answer:
• NMOS: Linear (ON).
• PMOS: Cutoff (OFF). $V_{out} = 0V$.

Q33 Define the Switching Threshold ($V_M$ or $V_{INV}$).

Answer: It is the point on the VTC curve where $V_{in} = V_{out}$. It is the exact threshold where the inverter decides a signal has flipped from 0 to 1 or 1 to 0.

Q34 How do you design an ideal "Symmetric" CMOS Inverter?

Answer: You want $V_M$ to be exactly $\frac{V_{DD}}{2}$. To achieve this, the strength (Beta, $\beta$) of the PMOS must equal the strength of the NMOS. $\beta_n = \beta_p$.

Q35 If $\mu_n \approx 2.5 \times \mu_p$, how do you size the transistors for a symmetric inverter?

Answer: Since holes are slower, the PMOS must be made physically wider to compensate.
$W_p = 2.5 \times W_n$ (assuming lengths $L$ are kept minimum and equal). This equalizes their drive strengths.

Q36 What happens to the VTC curve if you make the PMOS much larger than $2.5 \times W_n$?

Answer: The PMOS becomes stronger than the NMOS. It will hold the output High for a longer time as $V_{in}$ rises. The Switching Threshold ($V_M$) shifts to the RIGHT ($> V_{DD}/2$).

Q37 What happens to the VTC curve if you make the NMOS stronger (e.g., $W_n = W_p$)?

Answer: The NMOS pulls down the output much earlier. The Switching Threshold ($V_M$) shifts to the LEFT ($< V_{DD}/2$). This makes the inverter more sensitive to low input voltages (skews the noise margin).

Q38 Tricky What is a Skewed Inverter, and where is it used?

Answer: An inverter intentionally designed with unequal strengths (e.g., HI-skew or LO-skew).
• A HI-skew inverter (strong PMOS) is used on paths where a fast 0-to-1 rising transition is highly critical.
• A LO-skew inverter (strong NMOS) is used for fast 1-to-0 falling transitions.

Q39 Why does short-circuit current peak at $V_{in} \approx V_{DD}/2$?

Answer: At $V_{DD}/2$, the overdrive voltage ($V_{GS} - V_{TH}$) for BOTH transistors is large, and both are in saturation. They are both highly conductive, creating a direct low-resistance path between power and ground.

Q40 How can you minimize short-circuit power dissipation?

Answer: Ensure fast input rise and fall times (slew rates). If the input transitions very quickly through the $V_{DD}/2$ region, the transistors spend less time simultaneously ON, heavily reducing the short-circuit current integral.

4. Noise Margins & Signal Integrity

Q41 What are $V_{IL}$ and $V_{IH}$ on the VTC curve?

Answer: They are the unity gain points. They are the exact voltages on the input axis where the slope of the VTC curve is $\frac{dV_{out}}{dV_{in}} = -1$.
• $V_{IL}$: Max voltage recognized as a valid Logic 0.
• $V_{IH}$: Min voltage recognized as a valid Logic 1.

Q42 What are $V_{OL}$ and $V_{OH}$?

Answer: The output voltages guaranteed by the inverter when fed valid logic inputs.
• $V_{OL}$: Output low voltage (ideal CMOS = $0V$).
• $V_{OH}$: Output high voltage (ideal CMOS = $V_{DD}$).

Q43 Write the formulas for Noise Margin Low ($NM_L$) and Noise Margin High ($NM_H$).
$NM_L = V_{IL} - V_{OL}$
$NM_H = V_{OH} - V_{IH}$
Q44 What does Noise Margin physically represent?

Answer: It is the maximum voltage spike (noise from crosstalk, power supply dips, or EMI) that can be added to an input signal before the gate misinterprets the logic level and flips its output.

Q45 Why does a symmetric inverter ($W_p = 2.5 W_n$) have the best noise immunity?

Answer: Because $V_M = V_{DD}/2$, the curve is perfectly centered. This maximizes BOTH $NM_L$ and $NM_H$ equally. If skewed, one margin becomes large, but the other shrinks to a dangerous level, making the system vulnerable to noise on that specific transition.

Q46 Tricky Can $NM_L$ or $NM_H$ be negative?

Answer: No, a negative noise margin means the gate cannot even drive itself, let alone the next stage. The logic family would be fundamentally broken and useless.

Q47 If you cascade 10 identical CMOS inverters, does the signal degrade?

Answer: NO. Because CMOS acts as a signal restorative logic family. Even if an input comes in at $0.8V$ (poor zero), the inverter amplifies it back to a perfect $V_{DD}$. The VTC acts as a digital regenerator at every stage.

Q48 What is the "Forbidden Zone" in digital logic?

Answer: The voltage region between $V_{IL}$ and $V_{IH}$. If a signal rests in this zone, the inverter cannot reliably determine if it is a 1 or a 0. The output may oscillate, sit at an intermediate voltage, and burn massive short-circuit power.

Q49 How does lowering $V_{DD}$ (Voltage Scaling) affect Noise Margins?

Answer: As $V_{DD}$ drops (to save power), the absolute width of the VTC curve shrinks. This dramatically reduces the Noise Margins, making ultra-low-power IoT chips highly susceptible to random noise and crosstalk.

Q50 Whiteboard Why is the region between $V_{IL}$ and $V_{IH}$ considered high-gain?

Answer: Because a tiny change in input voltage ($\Delta V_{in}$) causes a massive swing in output voltage ($\Delta V_{out}$) from $V_{DD}$ to GND. The slope (gain) in Region C is much steeper than -1. This high gain is what ensures sharp digital transitions.
